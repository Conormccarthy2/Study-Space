Study Tips

Get started when you’re stuck, remember what you’ve learned, and practise for exams with a clear plan.

Open focus timer ↗

A 25-minute study plan

  1. 2 minPick one task and write down what done looks like.
  2. 18 minWork on that task and mark anything you get stuck on.
  3. 5 minClose your notes, recall the main points, and write down what to revisit.

Get started

When the workload feels too big.

Make the first step smaller

Turn “revise Python” into “trace one loop by hand.” If that still feels like a lot, start by opening a practice question and writing down what it’s asking.

Give yourself a finish line

Choose something you can tick off, like answering five questions or outlining three paragraphs. Add it to your to-do list before you start.

Park distractions for later

Keep a scrap of paper beside you. When you remember something unrelated, jot it down and return to your task. Check your list after the session.

Remember more

When the notes feel familiar but the answers don’t come easily.

Recall it before you reread it

Close your notes and answer two or three questions from memory. Check your answers, then turn anything you missed into a question for next time.

Come back to it later

Set a reminder to revisit the topic tomorrow and again later in the week. Start each review with a question you found tricky.

Explain it in your own words

Pick a concept and explain it out loud as if a friend missed the lecture. If you get stuck, check that part of your notes and try explaining it again with an example.

Prepare for exams

When you’re ready to see what you can do under pressure.

Time one past-paper question

Use the marks to decide how much time to give the question. When time’s up, compare your answer with the marking scheme and pick one thing to improve.

Keep a short mistake log

Write down what went wrong and what you’ll check next time. For example, “I skipped the empty-list case. Test it before I submit.” Retry the question a few days later.

Mix up your practice

Once you’ve practised each topic on its own, try a small set of mixed questions. Before solving each one, say which method you’d use and why.
